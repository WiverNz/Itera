# Post-MVP candidates: evidence review

Status: research for review, not an approved product claim. Reviewed and expanded 2026-11-26. Covers Prediction Testing, Environment Design, BLUF and a breathing-based focus pause. The existing directory is retained to preserve links.
Companion documents: [product and technical proposal](proposal.md), [draft milestone](milestone.md), [proposed ADR](../../architecture/adr/0023-prediction-testing-proposal.md).

## Combined conclusion

Prioritize a small **Environment Design extension** to existing techniques; retain **Prediction Testing** as the more distinctive but more expensive optional Reflection extension; consider a **gentle breathing pause** only as an optional supporting tool; defer **BLUF and a Communication skill** pending a broader product case. None presently justifies a new compulsory curriculum technique. These priorities reflect product fit and cost as well as evidence; they are not a ranking of clinical effect sizes.

The additional searches covered environmental restructuring, availability/proximity, implementation intentions, habit formation, BLUF/main-point-first writing, and controlled/slow/box breathing. Official writing standards establish professional practice, not experimental efficacy. Breathing studies primarily measure stress, mood or physiology, not task performance. Habit research largely concerns health behaviour, not knowledge-work preparation. No direct trial of the proposed Itera exercises was identified in this targeted search.

## Prediction Testing conclusion

Small, planned tests of specific predictions are an established cognitive-behavioural practice. There is enough rationale to explore a bounded productivity exercise, particularly for perfectionistic avoidance and inaccurate expectations about starting work. There is not enough direct evidence to claim that this standalone app exercise improves productivity, treats a disorder, permanently changes beliefs, or outperforms Itera's current techniques.

Recommend an optional extension to Daily reflection for evaluation before considering a fifteenth technique. This is a product inference, not a finding from a trial of Itera.

## Review method and limits

This is a targeted narrative review, not a systematic review or exhaustive search. Searches covered behavioural/behavioral experiments, comparisons with thought records and exposure, procrastination treatment meta-analyses, CBT for perfectionism, expectancy violation, self-efficacy, and human memory reconsolidation including replication failures. Sources were checked through PubMed, PMC, publisher/university repositories and the Western Australian Centre for Clinical Interventions (CCI). Search date is above; publication years below are publication years, not search-engine crawl dates.

Priority: systematic reviews of interventions; directly relevant comparative studies; established clinical methods; mechanistic research with explicit translational limits. Clinical anxiety evidence is included to understand the method, not to broaden Itera into anxiety treatment. Commercial neuroscience explanations, popular productivity claims and testimonials are excluded. Some papers were available only as abstracts; these are marked. No pooled estimate was calculated, no formal risk-of-bias assessment was performed, and absence of a study in this search is not proof that none exists.

## Practical relevance by problem

These are qualitative applicability judgments, not formal evidence grades. No row has direct validation of the proposed Itera flow.

| Everyday problem | Plausible test | Evidence proximity and caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Procrastination | Predict whether a tiny task can be completed before feeling motivated; try and record the outcome. | Indirect package-level evidence and established practice resources. Useful when a testable expectation blocks action; cannot repair missing time or resources. |
| Perfectionism | Produce a private rough paragraph with a fixed editing limit; inspect whether it contains one usable idea. | Strongest practical fit with CBT-for-perfectionism literature, but component-specific benefit remains uncertain. Never reduce necessary safety or professional checks. |
| Fear of mistakes | Test whether a deliberately rough private draft can be revised into something usable. | Related clinical evidence; everyday adaptation is an inference. No deliberate mistakes in live transactions, health, legal or safety work. |
| Avoiding difficult tasks | Predict progress on one bounded subtask, then observe it. | Plausible behavioural learning; action and task decomposition may explain benefit without belief change. |
| Low confidence | Predict ability to complete one specific step with available support. | Self-efficacy rationale; avoid global judgments such as “I am capable/incapable.” |
| Overthinking | Set one decision criterion for a reversible personal choice, decide, then check that criterion. | Weak direct evidence for this use. Planning and repeated checking can themselves become avoidance; permit stopping without certainty. |
| Unrealistic effort/difficulty expectations | Estimate minutes for a familiar small task, measure once, and compare in the same units. | Strong face validity for calibration, but improved estimates or productivity from this implementation are untested. One observation is context-specific. |

## Mechanisms: what can and cannot be said

**Behavioural experiments:** specify an expectation, act under bounded conditions, observe, interpret. Unlike a motivational cue, the exercise explicitly permits the prediction to be supported. Unlike ordinary retrospective journaling, the original prediction is recorded before the result is known. This prospective comparison is the proposed product distinction, based on the methods above.

**Prediction error:** the difference between expected and observed outcomes is useful descriptive language. The app should say “compare what you expected with what happened.” It does not measure a neural prediction-error signal, dopamine, or memory destabilization. Surprise need not occur and is not a success criterion. The clinical learning account in [Craske et al.](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5x26t4nd) motivates inquiry but does not validate a productivity protocol.

**Experiential learning and belief updating:** action plus reflection is a plausible instructional rationale, not a claim that one learning-cycle theory uniquely explains change. A new observation can justify a narrower prediction, leaving confidence unchanged or increasing it. No Bayesian posterior should be computed from one self-report. A single trial with uncontrolled conditions cannot establish causation; a second safe trial may clarify whether the lesson transfers.

**Memory reconsolidation:** exclude it from user-facing explanations and scheduling. The positive laboratory result, direct replication failure and critical reviews above jointly give no basis for promising permanent rewriting, guaranteed emotional change, or a universal five-hour window. Morning preparation and evening review are convenience choices; an outcome can be reviewed immediately or days later. No emotional reactivation or trauma recall is needed.

## Claim boundaries and evaluation

Suitable original explanation: “Write down what you expect, try one small action, and compare it with what happens. The result can help you decide what to try next.” This states the procedure and a possibility, not an efficacy guarantee.

Reject: “rewire your brain,” “erase limiting beliefs,” “prove your fear wrong,” “cure procrastination,” “change your emotional memory permanently,” and any timing deadline justified by neuroscience. Do not equate lower conviction, better mood, or a positive outcome with successful practice.

Before a standalone technique is justified, compare the proposed experience with ordinary Daily reflection on usability and useful learning: can users formulate an observable prediction, distinguish evidence from interpretation, resume later, and identify a practical next step? Include supported and inconclusive outcomes. A small qualitative study can establish comprehensibility and burden, not efficacy. Any future efficacy claim requires an appropriately designed comparative study with behavioural outcomes and follow-up; positive testimonials or pre/post confidence shifts are insufficient.

## Environment Design / Friction Reduction

Distinguish three operations: changing physical/digital opportunity (tools within reach, fewer setup steps); deciding a cue and response in advance (an implementation intention); and repeating behaviour in a stable context (habit learning). They may work together, but preparing once is not evidence that a habit has formed. “Friction” is useful plain language for effort, access and inconvenience, not a single established scientific treatment with one effect size.

[Implementation intentions and goal achievement](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1) | Across 94 independent tests, if–then plans were associated with better goal attainment (d=0.65). | This is evidence about specifying when/where/how, not about equipment preparation alone. | Singh et al. (2024), systematic review/meta-analysis; abstract, methods and results inspected. [Time to Form a Habit](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488), DOI 10.3390/healthcare12232488 | Twenty studies, 2,601 participants; repeated health behaviours, contextual and preparatory factors were examined. **Product inference:** this has high practical fit and high overlap. Ask users to remove one actual obstacle, not to redesign their life. Evening preparation fits the existing tomorrow-change field, but “before your next attempt” is more inclusive of shift work and irregular schedules. Evaluate whether the preparation was usable and reduced setup effort at the next opportunity. “No opportunity,” “no difference,” and “made it harder” are valid results. A single next-day comparison is informative reflection, not a controlled causal test.

Making distractions less convenient is reasonable when reversible and user-chosen; do not block emergency access, remove assistive tools, impose deprivation or require disabling security. This boundary is a product safety choice, not a finding from the food studies. Useful claim: “Put what you need within reach and remove one obstacle before your next attempt.” Reject claims that willpower is irrelevant, a tidy desk guarantees focus, or a habit forms after a fixed number of days.

## BLUF / Main point first

BLUF is an established communication convention: place the conclusion, decision or request before supporting explanation. Official [US Army writing guidance](https://home.army.mil/wood/index.php/download_file/view/6631/1525) explicitly identifies the main-point-first approach as BLUF (indexed document excerpt inspected). [Digital.gov's plain-language organization guidance](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles/organize/) recommends beginning with purpose and important information, while noting that process instructions often need chronological order (page inspected). These are authoritative practice sources, not randomized studies.

A targeted search for BLUF/main-point-first communication and comprehension trials did not identify a robust BLUF-specific systematic review or a direct trial of the proposed professional rewriting exercise. This is a search limitation, not a claim that no empirical work exists. Broader readability or plain-language research cannot isolate the effect of moving the conclusion to the first line. Do not market a proven response-rate or productivity improvement.

**Product inference:** immediate utility is plausible for requests, decisions, email summaries and status updates, and the output is inspectable without AI. It differs from Feynman: Feynman checks one's understanding through explanation; BLUF helps a particular reader find the intended message/action. But communication is not one of Itera's five current skills. One writing convention does not establish enough curriculum depth or user demand for a sixth skill.

Use reader comprehension as the criterion: can the intended reader identify the message, action and essential caveat? Concision must not delete uncertainty, evidence, courtesy or required procedural order. A sensitive message may need a brief acknowledgement before its main point. A direct request is not permission to sound coercive. Cross-language and workplace conventions require local review; an English military acronym need not appear in the user's rewritten text.

## Box breathing versus a gentle breathing pause

| Source and access | Relevant evidence | Limits for Itera |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fincham et al. (2023), RCT meta-analysis; abstract, methods, bias and safety sections inspected. [Effect of breathwork on stress and mental health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/), DOI 10.1038/s41598-022-27247-y | Twelve stress-outcome trials, N=785; pooled g=−0.35 (95% CI −0.55 to −0.14). Most interventions focused on slow breathing. | Risk-of-bias concerns, heterogeneous interventions, limited follow-up and sparse adverse-event reporting. Neither a box-breathing estimate nor evidence for a one-minute productivity reset. Clinical symptom outcomes should not become Itera claims. |
| Zaccaro et al. (2018), systematic review; abstract/full-text overview inspected. [Psycho-physiological correlates of slow breathing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/), DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353 | Reviews slow breathing below ten breaths/minute and associated physiological/psychological changes in healthy participants. | Heterogeneous methods and correlational mechanistic findings do not establish an optimal pace, dose or improved work output. Physiological changes are not proof of a “nervous-system reset.” |
| Yilmaz Balban et al. (2023), exploratory remote randomized study; results, limitations and participant sections inspected. [Brief structured respiration practices](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/), DOI 10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895 | Compared five minutes daily for about a month: mindfulness, cyclic sighing, box breathing and cyclic hyperventilation with retention. Of 108 enrolled, 21 were assigned box breathing. The clearest between-group positive-affect advantage versus mindfulness was for cyclic sighing; box breathing's difference was not significant. | Retrospective registration, small groups, remote adherence uncertainty and health exclusions limit inference. Within-group improvement is not proof of superiority. The study did not compare box breathing with a simple equal inhale/exhale no-hold guide, or test a 60-second work warm-up. |
| NHS, [Breathing exercises for stress](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/) (page inspected); Ashford and St Peter's NHS, [Relaxation, Breathing, and Mindful Movement](https://asph.nhs.uk/nervous-system-regulation) (indexed excerpt; direct fetch unavailable) | Official guidance emphasizes comfortable, unforced breathing; the latter advises stopping and returning to normal breathing if dizzy, light-headed or uncomfortable. | Practical safety guidance, not a guarantee of universal tolerability or evidence for the proposed animation/duration. |

**Comparison:** box breathing uses equal inhale, hold, exhale and hold phases; slow no-hold breathing uses two phases. There is no adequate evidence here that retention adds benefit for everyday focus. Prefer a gentle no-hold guide because it is simpler and avoids making breath retention a required task, not because a head-to-head trial has proved it superior. Cyclic sighing is distinct from merely lengthening the exhale; its results must not be borrowed to advertise a different protocol. Do not add hyperventilation, long holds or a maximal breathing test.

“Focus Reset” can imply a guaranteed physiological or attentional reset. Prefer **Breathing pause**, with “optional pause before focus” as its role. A 1–3 minute design is a convenience hypothesis to test, not the dose established by the studies. A user can start work without first becoming calm. No health metrics, stress score, treatment claim or insistence on repeating until an emotional change occurs.

## Cross-candidate evidence judgment

| Candidate | What is supported or established | What remains unproven | Smallest defensible product form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Environment Design | Context/convenience can affect behaviour; advance cue–action planning has intervention evidence | Exact preparation flow, evening superiority, work-output gain, habit formation from one setup | Examples and optional preparation prompts in existing techniques |
| Prediction Testing | Established CBT practice and indirect comparative/package evidence | Standalone Itera efficacy and incremental benefit over reflection | Optional structured Reflection extension after evaluation |
| BLUF | Established professional and plain-language convention | BLUF-specific causal productivity gains and demand for a Communication curriculum | Defer shipping; evaluate a local writing exercise only if demand emerges |
| Breathing pause | Some controlled evidence for stress/mood outcomes across breathing interventions | Box superiority, value of holds, effect of a short pause on task performance | Optional no-hold support tool, lower priority |

Do not compare effect sizes across these unrelated outcomes/populations as if they measured “productivity.” The proposed roadmap is based on fit, distinct value, user burden and maintenance cost. Sources inform design choices; they do not validate the proposed software.
